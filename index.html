How I work

Four habits, each backed by real work. Throughout this page, the notes on the right say what was measured and how.

  • Measure before changing. A warehouse migration started with a map of every dependency and a dry run of every rewrite, not with a guess.

    Mapped 211 views, 40 tables and 68 scheduled queries before touching any of them.

  • Reconcile every number. If two reports disagree, one of them is wrong, and I find out which.

    Attribution views cover 100% of revenue with no double counting. Automated campaign spend matched the Google Ads API at 98.9%.

  • Ship only what passes. A good demo isn't the bar. A model or agent goes live when it holds up on the checks that matter.

    Held back a purchase-propensity model with AUC 0.85 because it failed calibration. The agent platform we delivered passed 21 of 21 tenant-isolation checks.

  • Keep a way back. Changes to production data come with a diff that proves nothing moved, and a script that undoes them.

    55 rewritten warehouse objects shipped with byte-identical diff checks and rollback scripts.

Research

IEEE, 2026 International Conference on IT and Industrial Technologies (ICIT)

A Comparative Study of Transformer and Conditional VAE for Pakistan Sign Language Hand Pose Generation

Abdullah Arif, Amna Musharaf, Muhammad Saif ul Islam

  • Pakistan Sign Language has very little training data. We compared a Transformer with a conditional variational autoencoder for generating hand poses from gloss: which learns pose better, and which holds up on signs it hasn't seen.

    Transformer with 4 layers, 512-dimensional embeddings and 8 attention heads, against a multilayer cVAE. Both trained on a public PSL dataset, with keypoints normalized to remove differences between signers. Scored by mean squared error, cosine similarity and a qualitative review of poses.

Read the paper on IEEE Xplore

Zaban-e-Ishara: text and speech to Pakistan Sign Language

Final-year project, August 2025 to May 2026. With Amna Musharaf and Tahmooras Khan, supervised by Saif Ul Islam.

Type a sentence in English or Roman Urdu, or say it out loud, and watch it signed by a skeleton animation in real time. The hand-pose component became the paper above.

  1. Listen. Whisper transcribes spoken input to text; typed input starts at the next step.
  2. Reorder. NLTK rules move English word order toward PSL grammar.
  3. Translate to gloss. A fine-tuned T5-Base writes the PSL gloss. 89.6% accuracy with 4-fold cross-validation.
  4. Find or generate poses. Dictionary signs come from a pose lookup; out-of-vocabulary words go through a generative pathway.
  5. Animate. FastAPI and Next.js render a 225-point MediaPipe skeleton in the browser.
